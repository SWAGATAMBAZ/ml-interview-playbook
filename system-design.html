Production · The open-ended design round

ML system design

“Design a fraud detection system.” “How would you predict churn?” These 45-minute rounds test structured thinking more than any single algorithm. Use a repeatable framework, make trade-offs explicit, and go deep where the interviewer pulls. Four worked case studies below.

The 8-step framework

  1. Clarify the problem & goal. Who uses the predictions, what decision do they drive, what is success in business terms? Scale (users, QPS), latency, constraints (regulation, explainability, fairness), existing system/baseline.
  2. Frame as an ML task. Input → output; classification/regression/ranking; prediction unit and timing; label definition (and its delay and noise). Is ML even needed? Start with a heuristic baseline.
  3. Metrics. Offline (PR-AUC, NDCG, MAPE…) and online (conversion, loss prevented, CTR) + guardrails (latency, complaints, fairness).
  4. Data. Sources, labelling, volume, freshness, biases, privacy; how to build training sets without leakage (point-in-time).
  5. Features. Grouped by entity (user, item, context, interactions), real-time vs batch, embeddings; feature store.
  6. Model. Baseline → simple interpretable model → stronger (GBM / two-tower / sequence models); training cadence, imbalance handling, validation scheme.
  7. Serving & architecture. Batch vs online, candidate generation + ranking, latency budget, caching, fallbacks; draw the diagram.
  8. Evaluation, deployment & monitoring. Offline eval → shadow → A/B test; monitoring of drift, data quality, performance; retraining; feedback loops; iteration plan.
💬 Opening line

“Before I jump into models, can I clarify the goal and constraints? … Here's how I'll structure it: problem framing, metrics, data and features, model, serving, then evaluation and monitoring. I'll start simple and go deeper wherever you'd like.”

Case studies

Real-time card fraud detection

Clarify: block/flag transactions at authorisation; decision in < 100 ms; fraud rate ~0.1–0.5%; cost of missed fraud (amount + chargeback fees) vs false declines (lost sale, customer friction); review team capacity.

Framing: binary classification per transaction → risk score; actions by thresholds: approve / step-up auth (OTP) / decline / manual review.

Labels: chargebacks & confirmed fraud reports — delayed 30–90 days and noisy; declined transactions have no outcome (selection bias → keep a small random approve holdout or use reject inference).

Metrics: offline PR-AUC, recall at fixed FPR (e.g. 0.1%), fraud $ caught at review capacity (precision@k); online: fraud loss rate, false-decline rate, customer complaints; latency p99.

Features: transaction (amount, merchant category, channel, currency), velocity (count/sum in last 1 h/24 h per card/device/IP — streaming aggregates), deviation from the customer's habits (amount z-score vs history, new merchant/device/country), device fingerprint, geo distance from last transaction / impossible travel, merchant risk (target-encoded, smoothed), graph features (shared devices/cards between accounts).

Model: rules baseline → gradient boosting (LightGBM/XGBoost) with class weights; calibrated scores; optional graph/sequence models for rings; unsupervised Isolation Forest scores as extra features for new patterns.

Serving: streaming feature computation (Kafka + Flink) into an online feature store; low-latency model service (in-memory GBM, ONNX/Treelite); timeout fallback to rules; decision engine combining model score + rules + business policies.

Monitoring: score distribution & PSI per feature hourly, alert rates, analyst feedback precision, delayed-label performance; adversarial adaptation → frequent retraining (weekly) + rapid rule updates; champion/challenger.

Subscription churn prediction

Clarify: who acts on it (retention team offering discounts?), budget/capacity, how early do we need to know (30 days ahead?), definition of churn (cancellation vs inactivity for 30 days).

Framing: binary classification: “will this active customer churn in the next 30 days?”, scored weekly in batch. Better: uplift modelling — who will be persuaded by an offer (not just who will churn) to avoid wasting incentives on sure-stayers and lost causes.

Training set: snapshots at many past dates; features from data before the snapshot only; label from the following 30 days (point-in-time, no leakage); time-based validation.

Features: tenure, plan, price changes, usage trend (last 7 vs previous 28 days), engagement decline, support tickets & sentiment, payment failures, contract end date, discounts, cohort, NPS.

Model: logistic regression baseline (explainable drivers) → GBM; SHAP for reason codes the retention team can use; calibrated probabilities to compute expected value = P(churn) × CLV × P(save | offer) − offer cost.

Metrics: offline PR-AUC, lift / precision in the top decile; online: A/B test of targeted campaign vs control → retained revenue net of incentive cost.

Serving: weekly batch scoring into the CRM; dashboards; monitor drift after pricing changes; retrain monthly.

E-commerce product recommendations

Clarify: surface (home feed, “similar items”, email), catalogue size (10M items), users (50M), latency (< 200 ms), objectives (CTR, conversion, GMV, diversity, new-item exposure).

Architecture — multi-stage: (1) candidate generation from millions to ~1,000: collaborative filtering / matrix factorisation, two-tower embedding model with approximate nearest-neighbour search (FAISS/HNSW), co-purchase graphs, popularity/trending, content-based for new items; (2) ranking ~1,000 → 50 with a feature-rich model (GBM/LambdaMART or a deep ranking model) predicting P(click), P(purchase); (3) re-ranking for business rules, diversity, freshness, de-duplication, sponsored slots.

Data & labels: implicit feedback (impressions, clicks, add-to-cart, purchases) with position bias; negatives = shown but not clicked (and sampled); train on logged data with care for exposure bias.

Features: user (history embeddings, demographics, recency), item (category, price, popularity, embeddings from text/images), user–item (past interactions with category/brand, similarity scores), context (time, device, session).

Cold start: new users → popularity + onboarding preferences + session-based models; new items → content embeddings + exploration boost.

Metrics: offline recall@k (candidates), NDCG@k/MAP (ranking); online A/B: CTR, conversion, revenue/session, long-term retention; guardrails: diversity, coverage, latency.

Ops: embeddings/indices refreshed daily, ranking model retrained daily/weekly, real-time session features, bandits/exploration to avoid feedback loops.

Store–SKU demand forecasting

Clarify: decision = replenishment orders; horizon (next 14 days, daily), granularity (10k SKUs × 500 stores), lead times, cost of stock-out vs overstock (perishables!).

Framing: probabilistic regression per store–SKU–day; produce quantiles (P50, P90) because ordering decisions depend on service level.

Model: seasonal-naive & moving-average baselines → a global LightGBM across all series with lag features (7, 14, 28, 364 days), rolling means, price & promo flags, holidays, weather, store/SKU categoricals, Tweedie or quantile loss; hierarchical reconciliation (SKU → category → store); special handling for intermittent demand and new products (similar-item priors).

Validation: rolling-origin backtests over multiple cut-offs mirroring the 14-day horizon; metrics WAPE, bias, pinball loss; evaluate by segment (fast vs slow movers).

Serving: nightly batch pipeline (Airflow) → forecasts table → ordering system; forecast-value-add vs planners' overrides.

Monitoring: forecast accuracy per segment once actuals land, bias drift, stock-outs (censored demand: sales ≠ demand when out of stock — correct the training labels), promotion calendar changes.

What interviewers score

  • Asks clarifying questions and ties ML choices to the business objective.
  • Clear problem framing with a well-defined label and leakage awareness.
  • Sensible metric choices including online metrics and guardrails.
  • Starts simple and justifies added complexity.
  • Concrete, creative features grounded in the domain.
  • Understands production: latency, scale, freshness, monitoring, retraining, failure modes.
  • Communicates trade-offs and drives the conversation with structure.

Interview questions

How would you design a system to detect fake reviews?

Clarify the action (hide, downrank, review) and costs. Labels from moderation decisions and known fraud rings (noisy; use active learning). Features: text (embeddings, repetitiveness, sentiment extremity), reviewer behaviour (account age, review velocity, rating variance, burstiness on one product), graph signals (reviewer–product bipartite communities), metadata (IP/device sharing, verified purchase). Model: GBM on behavioural + text features, graph-based detection for rings. Metrics: precision at moderation capacity, recall on known rings; monitor adversarial shifts; human review loop.

How do you define the label for a churn model?

Work with the business: explicit cancellation for subscriptions; for non-contractual businesses, inactivity beyond a threshold chosen from the return-probability curve (e.g. 95% of users inactive for 45 days never return). Fix a prediction point and horizon (churn within 30 days after snapshot), exclude already-churned users, and keep feature windows strictly before the snapshot.

How would you handle the cold-start problem in recommendations?

New users: popular/trending items, onboarding questions, contextual signals (location, device, referrer), session-based recommendations that adapt within the first clicks. New items: content-based features/embeddings (text, image, category, price), exploration boosts or bandits to gather interactions, similar-item mapping.

How would you evaluate a recommender offline and online?

Offline: time-split holdout of interactions; recall@k for candidate generation; NDCG@k/MAP/MRR for ranking; coverage, diversity, novelty; counterfactual/off-policy evaluation with logged propensities (IPS) to correct exposure bias. Online: A/B test on CTR, conversion, revenue/session, retention, with guardrails (latency, diversity, complaints).

Batch-scored model or real-time API for a credit-limit increase campaign?

Batch: eligibility and offers are decided periodically, latency isn't critical, features come from monthly statements, and batch is cheaper, easier to audit and explain. Real-time only if the offer is triggered by in-session events.

How would you predict ETA for a food delivery app?

Decompose into stages (restaurant prep time, courier assignment & pickup, travel time) with separate models or a model with stage features; features: restaurant historical prep times by hour, current order load, courier location & availability, route distance, real-time traffic, weather; GBM with quantile loss for P50/P90; evaluate MAE and on-time %; online calibration; monitor during peaks and adjust quickly with real-time features.

How would you ensure fairness in a loan approval model?

Exclude protected attributes as inputs but test for proxies; measure fairness metrics across groups (approval-rate parity, equal opportunity/TPR parity, calibration within groups); analyse trade-offs with business and legal; mitigations: reweighting, constraints during training, group-specific thresholds where legal; explainability (reason codes, adverse-action notices); documentation (model cards), monitoring and periodic audits.

Your model will be used to rank search results. Which loss and metrics?

Learning-to-rank: pointwise (regression/classification of relevance), pairwise (RankNet, LambdaRank) or listwise (LambdaMART, ListNet). LambdaMART (LightGBM/XGBoost rank:ndcg) is a strong default. Offline NDCG@10/MRR from graded relevance labels or click models (correct position bias); online CTR, successful sessions, time to click, abandonment.
