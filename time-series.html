🎯 Say this in the interview

“I start with decomposition into trend, seasonality and residual, and a naive / seasonal-naive baseline. Classical models like ARIMA need a stationary series — constant mean and variance, autocovariance depending only on lag — which I check with ADF/KPSS and achieve by differencing or log transforms; p and q come from PACF and ACF cut-offs, or AIC search, and SARIMA adds seasonal terms. For many related series or rich covariates I use gradient boosting on lag, rolling-window and calendar features, being careful that every feature only uses information available at prediction time. Validation is always time-ordered — rolling-origin / TimeSeriesSplit with a gap — never shuffled K-fold. Metrics: MAE, RMSE, MAPE/sMAPE or MASE relative to the naive forecast.”

Components

  • Trend: long-run increase/decrease.
  • Seasonality: fixed-period patterns (weekly, yearly).
  • Cycles: irregular-period fluctuations (business cycles).
  • Noise/residual.

Additive \(y_t=T_t+S_t+R_t\) when seasonal amplitude is constant; multiplicative \(y_t=T_t\times S_t\times R_t\) when it grows with the level (log turns it additive). Tools: classical decomposition, STL (robust, flexible).

Stationarity

A (weakly) stationary series has constant mean, constant variance, and autocovariance that depends only on the lag. Most classical models assume it.

TestH₀Reading
ADF (Augmented Dickey–Fuller)Unit root (non-stationary)p < 0.05 → reject → stationary
KPSSStationaryp < 0.05 → reject → non-stationary

Use both (they have opposite nulls). Fixes: differencing \(y_t-y_{t-1}\) (trend), seasonal differencing \(y_t-y_{t-s}\), log / Box-Cox (variance), detrending.

ACF and PACF

  • ACF: correlation of the series with its lag k (includes indirect effects through intermediate lags).
  • PACF: correlation with lag k after removing the effect of lags 1…k−1.
ProcessACFPACF
AR(p)Tails off graduallyCuts off after lag p
MA(q)Cuts off after lag qTails off
ARMA(p, q)Tails offTails off

The ARIMA family

∑ ARIMA(p, d, q)
$$\text{AR}(p):\ y_t=c+\phi_1y_{t-1}+\dots+\phi_py_{t-p}+\varepsilon_t\qquad \text{MA}(q):\ y_t=\mu+\varepsilon_t+\theta_1\varepsilon_{t-1}+\dots+\theta_q\varepsilon_{t-q}$$

ARIMA applies ARMA(p, q) to the series differenced d times. SARIMA(p,d,q)(P,D,Q)ₛ adds seasonal AR/MA/differencing at period s. SARIMAX adds exogenous regressors (price, promotions, holidays).

  • Box–Jenkins: identify (stationarity, ACF/PACF) → estimate (MLE) → diagnose (residuals ≈ white noise: Ljung–Box test) → forecast.
  • Order selection by AIC/BIC (auto_arima).
  • Limits: one series at a time, linear, struggles with multiple seasonalities and many covariates.

Exponential smoothing, Prophet & others

  • Simple exponential smoothing: \(\hat y_{t+1}=\alpha y_t+(1-\alpha)\hat y_t\) — level only.
  • Holt adds trend (optionally damped); Holt–Winters adds seasonality (additive/multiplicative). ETS = state-space formulation with error, trend, seasonal components.
  • Prophet: additive model y = trend (piecewise linear/logistic with changepoints) + Fourier seasonalities + holidays; robust to missing data, easy for business series.
  • Croston / TSB for intermittent demand (many zeros).
  • Global ML / DL models: LightGBM on lags across thousands of SKUs; N-BEATS, TFT, DeepAR; foundation models (e.g. TimesFM, Chronos) for zero-shot baselines.

Multi-step strategies: recursive (feed predictions back in — errors accumulate), direct (one model per horizon), multi-output. Leakage traps: rolling windows that include the current value, target-encoded features computed on the whole series, scaling fitted on future data, random train/test splits.

Validation

Cross-validation schemes
Click TimeSeriesSplit: training always precedes validation.
  • Rolling-origin (expanding or sliding window) backtests mimic production retraining.
  • Add a gap equal to the feature look-back / label delay.
  • Match the validation horizon to the business horizon (next 7 days, next quarter).
  • Compare against naive (last value) and seasonal-naive baselines.

Interview questions

What is stationarity and why does it matter?

A weakly stationary series has constant mean and variance over time and autocovariance that depends only on lag. Models like ARMA assume it so that patterns learned from the past apply to the future; non-stationary series can produce spurious correlations and unreliable forecasts. Check with plots, ADF and KPSS; fix with differencing or transforms.

How do you choose p, d, q for ARIMA?

d: number of differences needed for stationarity (ADF/KPSS). p: lag where the PACF cuts off (AR order). q: lag where the ACF cuts off (MA order). Refine by AIC/BIC grid search (auto_arima) and verify residuals are white noise (Ljung–Box).

Why can't you use regular K-fold cross-validation for time series?

Shuffling puts future observations in training and past ones in validation, leaking future information and producing over-optimistic scores; autocorrelated neighbours also leak. Use time-ordered splits (expanding/sliding window, TimeSeriesSplit), with a gap for look-back features.

ACF vs PACF?

ACF at lag k is the correlation between y_t and y_{t−k}, including indirect effects through intermediate lags. PACF at lag k is the correlation after controlling for lags 1…k−1 — the direct effect. AR(p) shows PACF cut-off at p; MA(q) shows ACF cut-off at q.

How would you forecast sales for 10,000 products?

A global model (LightGBM) trained across all series with lag/rolling/calendar/price/promo features and product/store categoricals, predicting scaled or differenced targets; hierarchical reconciliation if forecasts must sum across levels; special handling for intermittent and new (cold-start) products; rolling-origin backtests; WAPE/MASE metrics; baselines per series.

What are the pitfalls of MAPE?

Undefined/infinite when actuals are zero, explodes for small actuals, asymmetric (over-forecasts can be penalised without bound while under-forecasts cap at 100%), and it favours models that under-forecast. Alternatives: WAPE, sMAPE, MASE.

Recursive vs direct multi-step forecasting?

Recursive: one 1-step model, feed predictions back as inputs — simple but errors compound. Direct: separate model per horizon — no compounding but more models and ignores dependencies between horizons. Hybrids and multi-output models sit in between.

How do tree models handle trend in time series?

Poorly — they can't extrapolate beyond the training target range. Remove trend first (differencing, ratio to a rolling mean, detrending with a linear model) and let the trees model the stationary remainder, or combine a linear trend component with tree residual models.

What is a unit root?

In y_t = ρ y_{t−1} + ε_t, ρ = 1 is a unit root: a random walk whose shocks persist forever and variance grows with time — non-stationary. ADF tests H₀: unit root. First differencing turns a random walk into stationary white noise.

How do you handle multiple seasonalities?

Fourier terms for each period as regressors (e.g. daily + weekly + yearly), TBATS, Prophet (multiple seasonal components), MSTL decomposition, or calendar features in ML models.

How do you produce prediction intervals?

From model-based standard errors (ARIMA/ETS), quantile regression (LightGBM quantile objective / pinball loss), bootstrapped residual simulation, or conformal prediction with time-aware calibration. Evaluate with coverage and interval width.
